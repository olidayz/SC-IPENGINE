# 2B-WORLD

Stage 2 of world expansion. The identity becomes an inhabitable place.

**Key Principle:** Everything here grows from Stage 1's DNA. World Texture is shaped by the **World Premise**. History explains how the premise came to be. Factions are incompatible answers to the Sacred Question — they absorb territory, characters, and conflicts into one structure. Objects crystallize the world into icons. Don't invent new identity — build the world that identity demands.

**For IP-Referenced Sparks:** The source IP likely already has locations, factions, characters, and objects. Use them. Expand, subvert, and deepen what exists. Invent new elements only where the source material has gaps. The goal is to reveal latent depth, not replace familiar territory with original worldbuilding.

---

## Speculative Layer (Optional Enrichment)

Now that the world's identity is grounded from Stage 1, ask: does this world benefit from a speculative element that deepens the premise? This is NOT about making the world "more sci-fi." It's about finding the one strange thing that makes the premise inescapable.

**Load:** `speculative-tech-seeds/SEEDS.md` → scan relevant categories for seeds that amplify the world premise. Use only if a seed genuinely deepens the existing identity. Most IP-Referenced worlds won't need tech seeds — the source IP's own strangeness is sufficient.

**Spacecadet dimension check:** Does the world naturally hit 2+ dimensions (Sci-Fi, Surreal, Spiritual, Satiraverse)? If yes, note silently and move on. If no, consider whether a speculative layer could add a missing dimension without distorting the premise. If the answer feels forced, skip it. Not every world needs all four.

**Format:** 2-3 sentences. "The world premise is [X]. The speculative layer that deepens it is [Y]. It affects [Z]." If no speculative layer is needed, state: "The premise carries without speculative enrichment" and move on.

---

## World Texture

What does it feel like to live in this world? Two paragraphs of atmospheric prose that set the stage for everything below.

**Paragraph 1: First Encounter.** What you see, hear, feel when you arrive. The sensory identity, the daily texture, how the economy shows up in ordinary life.

**Paragraph 2: Lived Experience.** What you learn after you've been here a while. The contradictions, the costs, the things that only make sense once you understand the rules.

**For IP-Referenced Sparks:** Start with the source IP's existing locations and aesthetics. What do they become when viewed through the spark's new lens? The familiar made strange.

---

## History

1-3 eras depending on the world's temporal depth. Some worlds have deep history. Some have one rupture point. Match the world, not the template. The past should haunt the present. History isn't backstory — it's active pressure on the current world.

**Process:** Each era should feel like a different genre. If the present is political thriller, the founding might be creation myth, and the rupture might be war epic. This gives the franchise tonal range across timelines. History should explain how the World Premise came to be — and if a Speculative Layer was added, how it entered or emerged.

**For IP-Referenced Sparks:** The source IP has real-world history (release dates, cultural eras, public perception shifts). That history can BE the world's history when reframed through the spark's lens.

Present as:

| Era | Period | Force | What Haunts |
|-----|--------|-------|-------------|

"Force" = the dominant agent of change in each era. May be a person, an institution, a technology, an event, or a movement.

---

## Factions

The world's engine. Each faction is an incompatible answer to the Sacred Question — a position a reasonable person could hold. None is simply "evil."

**Scaling:** 4-6 factions for political/institutional worlds. 2-3 for survival, psychological, or existential worlds. Fewer factions means deeper rows — territory and character columns do more work.

**Process:** Design factions first. Then give each one territory (where they operate), contested rivals (who they clash with and why), and a character archetype the faction produces (the individual human caught in this faction's gears).

**For IP-Referenced Sparks:** Existing characters and groups are faction seeds. What faction does each existing character naturally lead when the spark's lens is applied?

**Benchmark:** Dune (Great Houses with incompatible economic interests), GoT (families with valid but incompatible claims), Warhammer 40K (every faction playable AND antagonist), StarCraft (three philosophically distinct survival strategies).

**Anti-pattern:** Factions defined by aesthetic only ("the fire people" vs. "the ice people"). Factions must differ in worldview, not just appearance.

Present as:

| Faction | Want | Method | Territory | Contested By | Character It Produces |
|---------|------|--------|-----------|-------------|----------------------|

- **Territory:** Where this faction operates — geographic, institutional, or conceptual. Each territory should feel like a viable setting for an entire story.
- **Contested By:** Which faction(s), environmental forces, systemic pressures, or internal contradictions threaten this faction's hold. This is where conflict lives.
- **Character It Produces:** One archetype per faction — the person this world's systems generate. Not a story archetype (hero, mentor). A world-produced human with an internal tension, described in 1-2 sentences.

After the table, note any **Liminal Characters** — archetypes that don't belong to a single faction but move between them (archivists, diplomats, investigators, double agents). 1-2 sentences each, only if the world generates them.

---

## Conflicts

One paragraph. Name only the structural tensions that are NOT already visible from the Factions table's "Contested By" column. The factions table carries most of the conflict load — this paragraph catches the 2-3 deeper paradoxes, ticking clocks, or unresolvable dilemmas that sit beneath the faction-level disputes.

**Quality check:** Kill the villain. Does the world still have conflict? If yes, the system works. If no, you have a villain-dependent world.

---

## Objects & Artifacts

3-5 objects that define this world. Objects crystallize the world into something tangible — iconic, collectible, symbolic.

**For IP-Referenced Sparks:** The source IP likely has iconic objects already. What do they mean when viewed through the spark's new lens? The reframing IS the creative work.

**Quality check:** The signature object should be recognizable in silhouette. It should imply the entire world.

**Benchmark:** Lightsaber (Star Wars), Spice (Dune), Iron Throne (GoT), Red Pill (The Matrix)

Present as:

| Object | Significance |
|--------|-------------|

"Significance" = what the object costs AND what it means, in one description. The cost (what it takes to hold, protect, or use this thing) and the meaning (what it symbolizes about the world) belong in the same breath.

---

## Expansion Prompts

After presenting all sections, offer 3-4 contextual expansion options based on which areas of the world have the most latent depth. These should be specific to the world just built, not generic.

## Reference Check (Invisible)

After completing all sections, silently benchmark against `world-references.md`:

- **World Logic:** Do rules create meaningful tradeoffs? (Dune benchmark)
- **Role:** Are there 5-9 inhabitable identities? (Warhammer, Star Trek, StarCraft benchmarks)
- **Object:** Does the signature object stand next to the lightsaber? (Star Wars benchmark)
- **Conflict:** Are tensions independent of any single character? (Dune, StarCraft benchmarks)
- **Faction:** Are factions as philosophically distinct as StarCraft's three? (StarCraft, Warhammer benchmarks)
- **Anti-patterns:** Villain-dependency? Protagonist singularity? Faction flattening? Aesthetic over substance?

If any dimension is weak, flag it in prose when presenting. Don't display as a gate table.
