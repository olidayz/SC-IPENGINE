# Audio Plays — Sound Design for Short-Form

Sound is 50% of short-form. A video that works muted survives. A video that TRANSFORMS with sound-on goes viral. Design for both.

---

## The Dual-Layer Rule

Every video piece must work at two levels:

| Layer | Context | Must Deliver |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| **Muted** | 80%+ of social consumption. Autoplay in feed. Public transit. Work. | Visual clarity. Text overlays if needed. The hook lands without audio. |
| **Unmuted** | Active viewer. Headphones. Engaged. Chose to listen. | An entirely new dimension. Sound adds emotion, texture, or information the visual can't carry alone. The upgrade must be WORTH turning sound on. |

If muted and unmuted are the same experience, the sound is wasted. Sound must ADD, not repeat.

## Sound Categories

### 1. ORIGINAL SCORE
Custom music or sound design made for the content.

| Play | What It Is | Viral Potential |
|------|-----------|-----------------|
| **The Needle Drop** | One pre-existing song, placed perfectly. The song recontextualizes the image. The image recontextualizes the song. | Moderate — depends on licensing. But when it works, the audio-visual marriage IS the content. |
| **The Build** | Music that escalates. Starts minimal, layers accumulate. The audio escalation mirrors visual escalation. | High — the build creates physiological tension. Heart rate increases. Completion bait at biological level. |
| **The Drop** | Silence or minimal sound → sudden full audio. The drop syncs with a visual reveal. | Very high — the drop is the most reliable dopamine trigger in short-form audio. |
| **The Loop Score** | Music designed to seamlessly loop. End connects to beginning. The viewer doesn't notice the repeat. | High for loops — algorithmic completion rate benefits. |
| **The Contradiction Score** | Music that tonally contradicts the visual. Lullaby over violence. Heavy bass over tenderness. Children's song over horror. | Very high — tonal collision between audio and visual creates the most shareable content. |
| **The World Music** | Music that sounds like it comes FROM the IP world. Not a score ABOUT the world — music the characters would hear. | Medium-high — immersive. Builds world beyond the frame. Potential for audio trend if it's catchy. |

### 2. SOUND DESIGN
Non-musical audio. Texture, atmosphere, foley, designed sounds.

| Play | What It Is | When to Use |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| **The ASMR** | Hyper-detailed real-world sounds. Leather creaking. Scales scraping stone. Breathing. The microphone is TOO close. | Object reveals. Texture content. Craft processes. Loops. |
| **The Wrong Sound** | A familiar sound attached to the wrong source. A dinosaur that purrs. A saddle that hums. The mismatch between expected and actual sound. | World-building. Comedy. Unsettling content. |
| **The Silence** | Deliberate absence of sound in a moment that should have it. An explosion with no boom. A scream with no voice. The void IS the sound design. | Horror. Awe. Emotional weight. The silence after the drop. |
| **The Bass** | Sub-bass rumble felt more than heard. Physical. Tectonic. The phone vibrates. | Scale content. Dinosaur footsteps. Arrivals. Reveals. |
| **The Environment** | Ambient sound so specific it places you instantly. Wind through canyon rock. Distant animal calls. Rope against leather. Fire crackle. | Character intros. Micro-trailers. World establishing. |
| **The Glitch** | Digital artifacts — stutter, skip, bitcrush, distortion. The sound breaks. | Format subversion. Reality-breaking content. Channel surfing. Transitions. |

### 3. VOICE
Human voice — narration, dialogue, whisper, found audio.

| Play | What It Is | When to Use |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| **The Narrator** | A voice telling you something. Direct, authoritative, or intimate. The voice is the spine. | Micro-trailers (Speed Myth mode). Explainers. Lore delivery. |
| **The Whisper** | ASMR-quiet voice. Intimate. The viewer leans in. The volume IS the hook. | Secrets. Confessions. Character intros. |
| **The Found Voice** | Audio that sounds intercepted — radio static, phone recording, walkie-talkie. The format implies the voice wasn't meant for us. | World-building. Mystery. Found footage. Channel surfing. |
| **The Chant/Mantra** | Repeated phrase. Rhythmic. The repetition builds meaning. | Ritualistic content. Cult energy. Faction identity. Loops. |
| **The Single Line** | One line of dialogue. Nothing before. Nothing after. The line carries the entire concept. | Character intros. Hooks (the line IS the payoff). Stills with audio. |
| **The Voiceover Hijack** | A recognizable voiceover style (documentary narrator, true crime, nature doc, movie trailer voice) applied to IP content. The format clash is the comedy. | Format hijacks. Comedy. "In a world where..." applied to absurd premises. |

### 4. TRENDING AUDIO
Existing audio from the platform's trending sounds.

| Play | What It Is | Viral Potential |
|------|-----------|-----------------|
| **The Sound Ride** | Use a trending sound verbatim. Let the IP visual create the novelty. The sound provides algorithmic boost; the visual provides creative novelty. | High — algorithm favors trending audio. But the window is short (7-14 days). |
| **The Sound Subvert** | Use a trending sound but the visual CONTRADICTS the expected use. The sound says one thing; the image says another. | Very high — novelty within familiarity. The audience knows the sound and is surprised by the new pairing. |
| **The Sound Remix** | Take a trending sound and modify it — slow it, pitch it, chop it, layer it with world sounds. | Medium — depends on platform rules. Some platforms penalize modified trending audio. |
| **The Original Trend** | Create audio so compelling it BECOMES a trending sound. Other creators use YOUR audio over THEIR content. | Highest possible — but hardest. If it hits, every use of the sound extends your IP. |

## Audio Timing

### Beat Sync Rules

| Rule | Why |
|------|-----|
| **Cuts land on beats** | Music has rhythm. Edits have rhythm. When they sync, the viewer's brain registers the edit as intentional and satisfying. Offbeat cuts feel amateur. |
| **Text appears on beats** | Text popping onto screen must align with an audio event — beat, word, impact, silence break. Text without audio anchor floats. |
| **Reveals land on drops** | The most impactful visual moment syncs with the loudest/most dramatic audio moment. This is the emotional peak. If they're misaligned, both are weaker. |
| **Silence is a beat** | A gap in audio is an audio event. Use silence like a cut — it creates anticipation, focus, and weight. |
| **The first sound matters as much as the first frame** | In autoplay with sound on, the first 0.5 seconds of audio must hook. A jarring sound, a voice, a bass hit, a familiar melody — anything that makes the ear focus. |

### Pacing and Audio

| Content Speed | Audio Match |
|---------------|------------|
| **Fast cuts (1-2s)** | High BPM music, percussive hits on each cut, staccato sound design |
| **Medium pace (2-4s)** | Melodic music with structure, ambient beds with punctuation |
| **Slow/held shots (4s+)** | Drone, ambient, environmental sound, silence. The slow visual needs space in the audio. |
| **Escalation** | Rising pitch, increasing BPM, layering instruments, crescendo. The audio tells the body "something is coming." |
| **Deceleration** | Reverb tail, fadeout, pitch drop, single sustained note. The audio says "that just happened." |

## Audio Anti-Patterns

| Don't | Why |
|-------|-----|
| Use music as wallpaper | If the audio doesn't ADD meaning, it's noise. Every sound element must serve the content. |
| Default to epic/cinematic music | Short-form audiences are trained to skip "epic trailer music." It signals "ad" or "amateur." |
| Let audio fight the visual | If the visual is dense, the audio should be simple. If the visual is minimal, the audio can be complex. One must lead. |
| Ignore the muted experience | If your content is meaningless on mute, you've lost 80% of your potential audience before they even decide to engage. |
| Use trending audio past its window | A trending sound has a ~7-14 day window. After that, it signals "late." Early adoption = boost. Late adoption = penalty. |
| Put music over dialogue | If there's voice, the voice wins. Music goes under, way under, or disappears. Competing audio = neither lands. |
