# Pacing Science — Attention Architecture for Short-Form

How to hold attention second by second. Not theory — structural patterns for when to cut, when to hold, when to reveal, and when to let go.

---

## The Attention Curve

Short-form attention isn't a straight line. It's a series of COMMITMENTS.

```
ATTENTION
   ▲
   │  ┌─┐
   │  │ │    ┌──┐
   │  │ │    │  │         ┌───┐
   │  │ │    │  │    ┌─┐  │   │
   │──┘ └────┘  └────┘ └──┘   └──→ TIME

   0s  3s    7s       12s      END

   HOOK  COMMIT  RE-HOOK  COMMIT  PAYOFF
```

| Phase | Window | Job |
|-------|--------|-----|
| **Hook** | 0-1.5s | Stop the scroll. The first frame + first sound must arrest the thumb. |
| **Promise** | 1.5-3s | Tell the viewer why they should stay. Not explicitly — structurally. The content must IMPLY a payoff is coming. |
| **First Commit** | 3-5s | The viewer has chosen to watch. Reward the choice immediately — a small payoff, a reveal, a beat. |
| **Re-Hook** | 5-8s | Attention naturally dips at ~5-7 seconds. A new hook must land — tonal shift, new information, escalation, a question. |
| **Second Commit** | 8-12s | Deepened engagement. The viewer is invested. Content can slow slightly here — earned breathing room. |
| **Payoff** | Final 2-3s | The reason everything existed. The reveal, the punchline, the emotional peak, the reframe. Must be WORTH the wait. |
| **Loop Point** | Last frame → first frame | For loops and rewatchable content: the ending must create a reason to watch again. |

## Cut Timing

### The Cut Rhythm Library

| Rhythm | Pattern | Feel | When to Use |
|--------|---------|------|-------------|
| **Staccato** | 0.5-1.5s per shot. Rapid. Relentless. | Urgency. Overwhelm. Energy. Density. | Micro-trailers. Fever Dream mode. Escalation hooks. |
| **Waltz** | 2-3s per shot. Steady. Breathing room between beats. | Confidence. Style. Showing off. | Character intros. Gallery carousels. Measured reveals. |
| **Heartbeat** | Alternating long-short. 3s-1s-3s-1s. | Tension. Anticipation. Biological rhythm. | Horror. Suspense. Countdown energy. |
| **Avalanche** | Starts at 3s per shot. Accelerates to 0.5s. Never decelerates. | Escalation. Overwhelm. The density exceeds comprehension by the end. | Micro-trailers (Speed Myth). World reveals. |
| **Breath** | Long hold (4-6s) → rapid sequence (0.5-1s × 4) → long hold. | Contrast. The fast sequence earns its impact from the surrounding stillness. | Emotional content. Character intros with one burst of energy. |
| **Snare** | Even timing throughout — 2s, 2s, 2s, 2s — then ONE shot that's 0.5s. The break IS the beat. | The precision makes the irregularity hit harder. | Reveals. Punchlines. "Wait for it" moments. |
| **Free** | No consistent rhythm. Each shot exactly as long as it needs to be. | Organic. Dream-like. Unpredictable. | Fever Dream trailers. Art content. Experimental. |

### Shot Duration Rules

| Duration | What It Allows | What It Kills |
|----------|---------------|---------------|
| **0.3-0.5s** | A flash. Subliminal impression. Texture, not information. | Comprehension. The viewer FEELS it but can't describe it. |
| **0.5-1s** | One idea. One detail. One face. The minimum for conscious recognition. | Absorption. The viewer processes but can't dwell. |
| **1-2s** | A moment. Expression readable. Object identifiable. Action begun. | Boredom. At this speed, every shot must justify its length. |
| **2-4s** | A scene. Spatial relationships clear. Emotion lands. Body language readable. | Pace. Consecutive shots at this length feel SLOW in a feed. Earn it. |
| **4-6s** | A meditation. The viewer lives inside this shot. Detail emerges. | Energy. This duration is only viable after building trust through faster content, or as a deliberate breath. |
| **6s+** | A commitment. The viewer is watching a film, not scrolling a feed. Extremely rare in short-form. | Platform. Most algorithms begin penalizing static content after ~6s without a visual change. |

## The Re-Hook System

Attention decays. You must RE-HOOK the viewer at regular intervals. The re-hook is a moment that renews the viewer's commitment.

### 9 Re-Hook Techniques

| # | Technique | What It Does | Best at |
|---|-----------|-------------|---------|
| 1 | **The Cut** | A hard visual cut to a completely new composition. The newness itself re-engages. | Every 2-4 seconds in fast content |
| 2 | **The Reveal** | New information enters the frame — a detail, a face, a text overlay. | 3-5 second mark. 8-10 second mark. |
| 3 | **The Shift** | Tonal register changes. Serious → funny. Beautiful → disturbing. The contrast renews attention. | Midpoint of any piece. |
| 4 | **The Question** | A visual or textual question appears. The viewer's brain MUST answer. | When attention is dipping — usually 5-7 second mark. |
| 5 | **The Sound Event** | A new sound enters — beat drop, voice, silence break, environmental shift. Audio re-hooks are as powerful as visual. | Anytime, but especially effective at natural attention dips. |
| 6 | **The Scale Shift** | Dramatic change in framing distance. Close-up → wide. Or wide → extreme close. The spatial jump re-orients. | After 2+ shots at similar distance. |
| 7 | **The Movement** | Something that was still starts moving. Or something moving suddenly stops. Change in motion state. | When the frame has been static for >2 seconds. |
| 8 | **The Text** | Text appears on screen. The brain MUST read it. Involuntary attention. | Strategic moments. Don't overuse — text fatigue is real. |
| 9 | **The Human** | A face or body enters the frame where there wasn't one. Humans are involuntary attention magnets. | Whenever the content has been faceless for too long. |

### Re-Hook Frequency

| Content Length | Re-Hook Every |
|----------------|--------------|
| 5-10 seconds | 2-3 seconds |
| 15-30 seconds | 3-5 seconds |
| 30-60 seconds | 4-7 seconds |
| 60+ seconds | 5-10 seconds |

These are MAXIMUM intervals. Going longer without a re-hook risks losing the viewer. Going shorter creates density that can overwhelm — which is sometimes the goal (micro-trailers).

## Payoff Architecture

The payoff is the reason the content existed. It must be EARNED and DELIVERED.

### 7 Payoff Types

| Type | What It Delivers | Structure |
|------|-----------------|-----------|
| **The Reveal** | Information withheld now disclosed. | Curiosity → build → disclosure. The longer the wait, the bigger the reveal must be. |
| **The Punchline** | A comedic or conceptual landing. | Setup → misdirection → landing. Timing is everything. The punchline must arrive when the audience ALMOST expects it but slightly before. |
| **The Emotional Peak** | A feeling at maximum intensity. | Gradual escalation → peak frame. The peak must be a SPECIFIC emotion, not "emotional." |
| **The Reframe** | Everything you saw was actually about something else. | Normal content → one final detail/line/frame that recontextualizes everything before it. Rewatch bait. |
| **The Loop** | The end connects to the beginning. The payoff is the realization that it loops. | Build → arrival → reset. The viewer watches again to confirm the loop and catches what they missed. |
| **The Unresolved** | Deliberate non-ending. The payoff is the FEELING of wanting more. | Build → approach climax → cut. The absence of resolution IS the viral mechanic. Comments fill the gap. |
| **The Accumulation** | No single payoff. The density of everything together IS the impact. | Layer after layer → the overwhelm itself is the peak. Common in micro-trailers. |

### Payoff Timing

| Format | Payoff Position |
|--------|----------------|
| **Still** | Instant — the image IS the payoff. Or: the payoff is in the caption (reframe after the image). |
| **Loop (3-5s)** | The loop point IS the payoff — the moment you realize it's seamless. |
| **Hook (5-15s)** | Final 2-3 seconds. The LAST thing the viewer sees. |
| **Carousel** | Last frame. Every frame before it is setup. |
| **Micro-Trailer (15-45s)** | Usually: accumulated overwhelm. Sometimes: one final image that crystallizes everything. |
| **Character Intro (5-15s)** | The contradiction moment — the detail that reframes who you thought this person was. |

## Pacing Anti-Patterns

| Don't | Why |
|-------|-----|
| Start slow | The feed doesn't wait. Hook in frame 1 or die in the scroll. |
| Maintain constant speed | Monotonic pacing = flat engagement. Variation is oxygen. |
| Hold a shot because it's pretty | Pretty doesn't earn duration. Narrative need earns duration. |
| Cut fast because "that's what short-form is" | Fast cutting without purpose is noise. Every cut must serve the content's rhythm. |
| Save the best for last without earning it | If the viewer leaves at second 5, your incredible second-15 payoff never lands. Front-load quality. |
| Ignore audio pacing | Visual and audio rhythm must align or deliberately contrast. Unintentional misalignment feels broken. |
| Use the same cut rhythm throughout | Even a 10-second piece needs rhythm variation. Staccato throughout is numbing. One held moment inside fast cutting is electric. |
