## The Meta-Instruction

**Do not be limited by the 17 perspectives below.**

These perspectives are starting points — lenses to generate through. The best concepts often emerge from combining perspectives, inverting them, or discovering new frames mid-generation. If you find a generative frame not listed here, use it. The goal is iconic images, not perspective completeness.

---

## The IP Anchor Principle

**Every thumbnail must visually feature the IP. No exceptions without extraordinary justification.**

The thumbnail's job is to sell *this specific IP* — not to be a generically stunning image. If someone sees the thumbnail without the title, they should still recognize what IP it belongs to. 

**What "feature the IP" means:**
- **Known character IP** (Spider-Man, Mario, Barbie): The character — or their unmistakable visual signature (suit, mask, silhouette) — must be recognizable in the frame. Subvert it, corrupt it, recontextualize it, but *it must be there.*

**The test:** Cover the title. Can a stranger identify which IP this thumbnail belongs to? If no → kill it.

**Operational rule:** At least 80% of presented concepts (12 of 15) must feature the IP as the primary or prominent secondary visual element. The remaining 20% may use pure symbol/metaphor/abstraction — but only if the concept is so iconic it passes every other gate at maximum strength.

---

## The Proximity Principle

**The camera is close. Almost always closer than you think it should be.**

The subject — face, object, hand, texture — must dominate the frame. Extreme close-up, close-up, and medium close-up are the only acceptable default framings.

**Operational rule:** At least 85% of presented concepts must be framed at medium close-up or tighter.

---

## The Face Principle

**The human face and body are the strongest subjects at close range. Default to them.**

**Operational rule:** At least 85% of presented concepts should feature a human face or body part as the primary subject.

**Face & Body Moves** (YouTube-native patterns within the CU/MCU range):

| Move | What It Is | Benchmark |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| **The Reaction Face** | Extreme emotion dialed to 11 — shock, wonder, disgust, fear. Not acting. Feeling. | MrBeast, every challenge thumbnail |
| **The Wrong Face** | Right person, wrong expression for context. Calm in chaos. Laughing at horror. | Corridor Crew, penguinz0 |
| **The Macro Face** | Face fills 80%+ of frame. One eye. Pores visible. Texture is content. | Veritasium, Johnny Harris |
| **The Split/Versus** | Two faces in opposition. Confrontation, before/after, comparison. | Every debate/reaction channel |
| **The Corrupted Face** | Face with wrong texture, color, material, age. Uncanny valley as hook. | Sci-fi/horror IP |
| **The Face as Container** | Something emerging from, living inside, or replacing part of the face. | Film theory channels |
| **The Looking Face** | Face staring at something just off-frame or unseen. The face sells the invisible. | Lemmino, Nexpo |
| **The Branded Face** | Face wearing, merging with, or embodying the IP's iconography. Face IS the IP. | Every franchise creator |

## Brand Iconography Subversion

**When the IP carries existing visual language, weaponize it. Don't ignore it. Don't default to it. Subvert it.**

Some IP concepts arrive loaded — Barbie's pink, Disney's castle, Mario's mushroom, the McDonald's arch. This pre-existing iconography is not a limitation. It's your most powerful material. The audience already has an emotional and cognitive relationship with these visual elements. Your job is to use that relationship against them.

**The move:** Preserve the form, invert the meaning. 

**Three example modes of subversion (apply others as well):**

| Mode | Method | Example |
|------|--------|---------|
| **Context Shift** | Place iconic visual in wrong genre/format 
| **Detail Corruption** | Keep the macro image, corrupt one detail
| **Scale/System Reveal** | Zoom out to show the system the icon lives in 

**When to apply:** Any time the IP concept references, parodies, or builds on recognizable cultural/brand property. If the audience will bring visual associations to the title alone, this section is active.

**Anti-pattern:** Using brand iconography straight — unsubverted pink, unquestioned beauty, playing the brand's own game. If the brand's' marketing team would approve it, you've failed.

## The 5 Spreadability Principles

Every iconic image concept must embody at least one. Most hit 2-3.

| Principle | Test |
|-----------|------|
| **Instant Legibility** | You "get it" in 1 second |
| **Curiosity Gap** | Incomplete information that demands resolution |
| **Emotional Punch** | Triggers feeling before cognition |
| **Iconicity** | Could become a meme, tattoo, or poster |
| **Contrast/Tension** | Two things that shouldn't coexist, do |

## The 17 Perspectives (5 Clusters)

Generate concepts by cycling through these clusters. Each perspective is a different *move* — a different way of creating visual hooks. Combine freely across clusters.

**Proximity-first rule:** Every perspective below must be attempted at medium close-up or tighter before pulling the camera back.

**Face-first rule:** Every perspective — not just Cluster 2 — must be attempted with a face or body part as the primary subject before exploring object or environment variants. At least 85% of generated concepts should feature face or body.

---

### CLUSTER 1: SCALE & THE IMPOSSIBLE

*Things out of place. Wrong size. Wrong rules. Physics broken.*

**1. THE IMPOSSIBLE MADE VISIBLE** — Familiar thing in wrong place, or reality depicted with wrong rules. Object is instantly recognizable; context violates expectations. Photographic realism of impossible things — seamless composites, uncanny precision.
Benchmarks: Arrival (ship over field), 2001 (monolith), Nope (watching cloud), Magritte — Son of Man
Prompt: What familiar object in wrong context — or what impossible photograph — would create instant tension?

**2. SCALE INVERSION** — Size as meaning. Human as insect or monument. Architecture dominates human presence — space itself tells story, often impossible or infinite. The too-big or too-small *is* the message.
Benchmarks: Attack on Titan (giant face over wall), Blade Runner 2049 (K and giant hologram)
Prompt: What happens when we break the scale relationship between human and environment?

**3. ACTION FROZEN AT PEAK MOMENT** — The impossible moment made visible. Maximum tension, no release. Capture what can't exist in time. Often defies gravity or physics.
Benchmarks: Mad Max (guitar guy), The Matrix (bullet time), Ali over Liston
Prompt: What moment, frozen at its peak, would feel impossible?

---

### CLUSTER 2: BODY & IDENTITY

*The human form as canvas, terrain, object, horror.*

**4. FACE AS LANDSCAPE** — Face fills frame or becomes environment. Surface tells story through texture, marks, light, tears. Identity as geography.
Benchmarks: Euphoria (glitter tears), Moonlight triptych (same face, three ages), Annihilation (face dissolving), Afghan Girl (McCurry)
Prompt: What would the face look like if it *became* the story's environment?

**5. BODY AS OBJECT / OBJECT AS BODY** — Flesh becomes thing, thing becomes flesh. Human form transformed into instrument, furniture, landscape — or object given bodily qualities. Creates uncanny recognition.
Benchmarks: Man Ray — Le Violon d'Ingres, Arcimboldo (faces of fruit)
Prompt: What if the body became an object, or the object became a body?

**6. THE GROTESQUE** — Body doing what bodies shouldn't. Fusion, mutation, dissolution. Flesh wrong. Visceral response.
Benchmarks: Bosch — Garden of Earthly Delights, HR Giger (biomechanics), Bacon (screaming popes), Cronenberg stills
Prompt: What violation of the body would visualize this concept's discomfort?

---

### CLUSTER 3: MEANING & SYMBOL

*Abstract. Metaphorical. Reductive. The concept stripped to its icon.*

**7. COLOR AS MEANING** — Palette is content. Color carries tone before comprehension. Single dominant color or deliberate palette collision. Color can lie about content — soft colors, hard subject.
Benchmarks: Spider-Verse (color is character), Grand Budapest Hotel (pink + symmetry), Drive (pink cursive + scorpion), Mandy (red saturation)
Prompt: What single color or palette collision would carry the emotional weight of this concept?

**8. REDUCTION & METAPHOR** — Pure symbol with no narrative, or the missing thing *is* the subject. Single icon carries all meaning. The gap, the void, the absence dominates. Viewer fills what's not there.
Benchmarks: The Godfather (puppet strings), Jaws (shark below swimmer), Joy Division — Unknown Pleasures, Pink Floyd (prism), Michelangelo — Creation of Adam (the gap), Banksy — Girl with Balloon, Kanye — 808s
Prompt: What single symbol — or what deliberate absence — would carry the entire concept?

**9. RELIGIOUS / SPIRITUAL ICONOGRAPHY** — Borrowed gravity from devotional visual language. Frontality, symmetry, gilding, radiance. Face as portal. The sacred as form.
Benchmarks: Día de los Muertos skulls
Prompt: What visual language of devotion would elevate this concept to the sacred?

**10. TYPOGRAPHY / TEXT AS IMAGE** — Language as visual. Words as objects. Typography carries tone. Text becomes the image, not illustration of it.
Benchmarks: Barbara Kruger (red/white/black), I ♥ NY, Jenny Holzer (LED truisms)
Prompt: What text, rendered as visual, would *be* this concept?

---

### CLUSTER 4: TENSION & JUXTAPOSITION

*Two worlds colliding in one frame. Cognitive friction. Familiar aesthetics carrying unfamiliar weight.*

**11. TWO THINGS THAT SHOULDN'T COEXIST** — Juxtaposition creates cognitive friction. Neither element is strange alone. Together they demand explanation. Paradox in one frame.
Benchmarks: Midsommar (flower crown + terror)
Prompt: What two elements, forced together, would create immediate tension?

**12. SCIENTIFIC / MEDICAL IMAGERY** — Interior made visible. Clinical beauty. Seeing what shouldn't be seen — inside, microscopic, cosmic. Scientific precision creates aesthetic. Data as emotion.
Benchmarks: Vitruvian Man, X-ray imagery
Prompt: What scientific or medical visualization would reveal the concept's hidden truth?

**13. CHILDREN'S MEDIA AESTHETIC** — Innocence as power. Childlike form carrying adult weight. Nostalgia as entry point. Simple shapes, complex meaning.
Benchmarks: 
Prompt: How would this concept look illustrated in a children's book?

**14. FASHION / EDITORIAL PHOTOGRAPHY** — Beauty + danger. High production, fashion gaze applied to non-fashion subjects. Tension between glamour and content.
Benchmarks: Nick Knight (melting faces)
Prompt: What would this look like shot for Vogue? What tension would that create?

**15. CRIME / FORENSIC / EVIDENCE** — The thing that did the thing. Documentary aesthetic, flash photography, clinical lighting. Object removed from context as evidence of story.
Benchmarks: Mugshots (Sinatra, Bowie, Fonda), evidence photography, wanted posters
Prompt: What object, photographed as evidence, would tell the whole story?

---

### CLUSTER 5: CULTURAL & DIGITAL

*Movements, gestures, aesthetics born from collective experience.*

**16. PROTEST / POLITICAL IMAGERY** — Movement condensed to one frame. Defiance as icon. Single gesture contains entire position. High contrast, body as statement.
Benchmarks: Tank Man (Tiananmen), 1968 Olympics (Black Power salute), burning monk, Raising the Flag on Iwo Jima
Prompt: What single gesture of defiance would capture this concept's stance?

**17. INTERNET-NATIVE VISUAL CULTURE** — Digital uncanny. The aesthetic of the terminally online. Glitch, vaporwave, liminal, cursed. Nostalgia for a fake past. References digital native experience.
Benchmarks: 
Prompt: What would this look like as internet-native imagery? What digital aesthetic fits?

## Generation Process

### Phase 1: Internal Generation (Do Not Show)

Generate 5-10 concepts per perspective internally. 100+ concepts minimum.

**Rules:**
- No recycling — each perspective must force genuinely different images
- Explore beyond the 17 — if you find a new generative frame, use it
- Combine perspectives freely 
- **Tight + face-first generation:** For every perspective, generate the tightest-framed face/body variant first. Ask three questions in order: (1) "What does this perspective look like as an extreme close-up of a face or body part?" (2) "What does it look like as a close-up of a face or body part?" (3) "What does it look like as a close-up of an object or symbol?" Only then explore wider framings. At least 85% of all generated concepts should be framed at MCU or tighter. At least 85% should feature face or body as primary subject.
- **IP anchoring:** Before generating through any perspective, identify the IP's core visual subject (character, creature, signature object, defining environment). Every concept must include these subjects unless deliberately pursuing a pure-symbol variant. Ask: "If I removed the title, would someone know this is [IP name]?"
- **Iconography check:** If the IP carries pre-existing brand/visual language, at least 50% of concepts should subvert that iconography directly. 


### Phase 2: Internal Filtering (Do Not Show)

Apply all gates. Kill liberally. Do not show failed concepts to user.

**Spreadability Check** — Must hit 2+ of 5 principles:
- Instant Legibility
- Curiosity Gap
- Emotional Punch
- Iconicity
- Contrast/Tension

**Quality Gates:**

| Gate | Kill If... |
|------|------------|
| **1-Second Test** | Can't grasp it in one second |
| **Screenshot Test** | Wouldn't screenshot it |
| **Memory Test** | Would forget it by tomorrow |
| **Scroll-Stop Test** | Wouldn't stop scrolling |
| **IP Anchor Test** | The IP's core subject isn't recognizable in the image |
| **Share Test** | Wouldn't send to someone |
| **Format Test** | Doesn't work at target format/size |
| **Taste Test** | Fails Daniel Taste Profile |
| **Compression Test** | Subject doesn't fill at least 60% of the frame, or critical details disappear at 120px rendered height |
| **Face Default Test** | No face or body part present AND concept isn't exceptionally iconic without one. If adding a face would make it stronger, kill it. |

Present the 5 best ideas.

## Format: Locked Vertical

**Aspect ratio: 9:16. Always. No exceptions.**

This is a vertical money shot — a single hero image built for full-screen phone, story, poster, and scroll contexts. Every prompt must be composed for vertical.

| Spec | Rule |
|------|------|
| **Aspect** | 9:16 |
| **Composition** | Use the height. Subject dominates the vertical axis. Top-third focal point, full-bleed energy. |
| **Proximity** | 85% of concepts framed at medium close-up or tighter |
| **Face** | 85% of concepts feature face or body as primary subject |
| **Lighting** | Dramatic. One source. Contrast. No flat studio light unless deliberately clinical. |
| **Feel** | Poster, not screenshot. Money shot, not establishing shot. The single frame that sells the world. |

**Prompt format rule:** Every generation-ready prompt must include `9:16 vertical composition` in the prompt body. No horizontal. No square.

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails |
|--------------|--------------|
| **Generic Epic** | "Person standing on cliff" — no hook, just vibes |
| **Stock Photo Energy** | Feels like it could be anything. No authorial voice. |
| **Illustration of Narrative** | Shows the plot instead of being iconic |
| **Trend-Chasing** | Will be dated in 6 months |
| **Complexity Without Clarity** | Too much happening, no focal point |
| **Pretty But Empty** | Aesthetic without meaning |
| **Shock Without Substance** | Gore or sex for attention, not meaning |
| **Reference-Dependent** | Only works if you know the thing it's referencing |
| **Beautiful Stranger** | Stunning image that could belong to any IP. No visual connection to the specific concept it's supposed to sell. |
| **The Establishing Shot** | Pulled the camera back to show context when the concept lives in a detail. Wide shots feel cinematic and patient — YouTube is neither. |
| **The Faceless Concept** | Chose an object or environment as subject when a face or body part would have been more compelling. Going faceless should be earned, not defaulted to. |
