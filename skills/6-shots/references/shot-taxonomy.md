# Shot Taxonomy — Expanded

200+ tagged shot types. Organized by category. Each shot has a tag, description, emotional function, 9:16 vertical notes, and film reference where applicable.

---

## How to Use

**Tags:** CAPS format (e.g. `ECU-HAND`, `COWBOY`, `DUTCH-LOW`). Used in prompt tables.
**Browse:** By category header.
**Search:** By emotional need (index at bottom) or by scene type (index at bottom).

|-----|------|----------------|-------------------|------------|-----------|

## Category 2: CLOSE-UPS
*Face, feature, or object in immediate context. The standard unit of emotional cinema.*

| Tag | Name | What's in Frame | Emotional Function | 9:16 Notes | Reference |
|-----|------|----------------|-------------------|------------|-----------|
| `CU-FACE` | Face Front | Forehead to chin, full face, front | Emotion. The default. The face IS the story. | Face upper two-thirds. Below = vulnerability, above = weight. | Every great film uses this |
| `CU-PROFILE` | Face Profile | Face from side — one eye, jaw, ear | Contemplation, unawareness, being observed | Profile one third. Other two-thirds is what they see. | *In the Mood for Love* — Maggie Cheung profiles |
| `CU-3/4` | Three-Quarter Face | Face between front and profile — both eyes visible, one side favored | The most flattering and most common portrait angle. Slight mystery — one side is turned away. | Face fills upper frame. The favored side gets more light. | *The Godfather* — Brando's 3/4 lit face |
| `CU-UPSHOT` | Face from Below | Face from below chin — nostrils, underside of jaw, throat | Threat, power, judgment from above. The subject looks down at the camera/audience. | Face fills upper frame, the chin and throat are prominent. | *Do the Right Thing* — characters looking down at camera |
| `CU-DOWNSHOT` | Face from Above | Face from above — forehead prominent, eyes looking up | Vulnerability, submission, plea, childlike quality | Face in lower frame, forehead and hair prominent above. | *Schindler's List* — faces looking up |
| `CU-NAPE` | Back of Neck | Nape, hairline, collar, back of head | Vulnerability. The unguarded angle. What the subject can't protect. | Nape upper third. | *Lost in Translation* — Charlotte's neck |
| `CU-HANDS-LAP` | Hands in Lap | Both hands resting, fidgeting, gripping in the lap | Truth below the chin. What the hands do while the face performs composure. | Hands lower third. Lap and clothing context. | *Marriage Story* — hands during arguments |
| `CU-DETAIL` | Costume Detail | Watch, pin, earring, scar, button, badge | The signature detail. Changes how you read the character. | Detail centered. Body provides context. | *No Country for Old Men* — Chigurh's bolt gun |
| `CU-SHOULDER` | Over-Head Shoulder | Head and one shoulder, slightly behind | Intimacy from behind. We almost see what they see. | Head upper third, shoulder one side. | *Children of Men* — over-shoulder continuous shots |
| `CU-COUPLE` | Two Faces Close | Two faces very close together — foreheads touching, whispering, confronting | Maximum interpersonal intimacy or tension | Stack vertically in 9:16. One face above, one below. The gap between is millimeters. | *Portrait of a Lady on Fire* — face proximity |
| `CU-REACTION` | Reaction Shot | Face of the person LISTENING, not speaking | The reaction is often more important than the action. The listener IS the audience. | Face fills frame. Eyes directed off-screen toward the speaker. | *The Godfather* — Kay's face during Michael's lies |

## Category 3: MEDIUM SHOTS
*The body in its immediate context. Gesture visible, environment suggested.*

| Tag | Name | What's in Frame | Emotional Function | 9:16 Notes | Reference |
|-----|------|----------------|-------------------|------------|-----------|
| `MS-STANDARD` | Standard Medium | Waist up, centered | The workhorse. Body language and face both visible. | Subject fills vertical center. Headroom above, gesture below. | Universal |
| `MS-LOOSE` | Loose Medium | Waist up, subject smaller, more environment | The person IN the space. The room matters as much as the face. | Subject one third. Architecture fills rest. | *Succession* — characters small in expensive rooms |
| `MS-TIGHT` | Tight Medium | Chest up, cropped close | Between CU and MS. Conversation distance. The talking-heads shot. | Subject fills most of frame. | *The Social Network* — deposition scenes |
| `MS-COWBOY` | Cowboy Shot | Mid-thigh up — named for framing gunslingers' holsters | Action readiness. The body prepared. More dynamic than standard medium. | The extra leg room gives the frame energy. Vertical handles this well. | *The Good, the Bad and the Ugly* — duel framings |
| `MS-TWO-SHOT` | Two Shot | Two people waist up | Relationship. The gap between them IS content. | Stack (one behind other) or split the vertical frame. | *Before Sunrise* — Hawke and Delpy in conversation |
| `MS-DIRTY` | Dirty Single | One in focus, another's body part intruding at frame edge | Someone else is present. The intrusion creates triangulation. | Main subject center, dirty element entering from edge. | *Heat* — the diner scene |
| `MS-OVER-TABLE` | Over Table | Two people across a table surface — the table IS the arena | Negotiation, interview, interrogation. The table is the battlefield. | Table surface bisects vertical frame. One person above, one below, or both at edges. | *Heat* — Pacino/De Niro diner |
| `MS-SEATED` | Seated Medium | Waist up, character seated — chair/sofa visible | Settled, contained, domesticated. The seated posture IS the character state. | The chair contextualizes. Folding chair ≠ throne ≠ sofa. | *The Big Lebowski* — the Dude on his rug |
| `MS-STANDING` | Standing Medium | Waist up, character standing — posture and weight distribution visible | Active, ready, alert, performing. Standing is a choice when sitting is available. | The standing figure is taller in the vertical frame. More commanding. | *There Will Be Blood* — Plainview standing over people |

## Category 5: ANGLE SHOTS
*Camera position relative to subject. Power dynamics encoded in geometry.*

| Tag | Name | Position | Emotional Function | 9:16 Notes | Reference |
|-----|------|---------|-------------------|------------|-----------|
| `ANG-EYE` | Eye Level | Subject's eye height | Neutral, equal, observational | Eyes at upper third line. The default. | Universal |
| `ANG-LOW` | Low Angle | Below subject, looking up | Power, authority, threat, grandeur | Subject towers. Sky/ceiling above. | *Citizen Kane* — Kane from below |
| `ANG-HIGH` | High Angle | Above subject, looking down | Vulnerability, smallness, surveillance | Subject small, floor surrounds. | *Psycho* — shower scene overhead |
| `ANG-OVERHEAD` | Bird's Eye | Directly above, straight down | God's-eye. Pattern recognition. Judgment. | Ground IS the frame. Objects as diagram. | *Moonlight* — overhead water shots |
| `ANG-GROUND` | Ground Level | Camera on floor/ground | The space from below. Shoes, debris, undersides. | Floor stretches up. Subject towers. | *The Shining* — Danny's Big Wheel POV |
| `ANG-WORM` | Worm's Eye | Ground, pointing nearly straight up | Extreme low. Ceilings, chins, awe, claustrophobia. | Ceiling fills most of frame. | *The Third Man* — low angles in Vienna |
| `ANG-DUTCH` | Dutch / Canted | Tilted on axis | Unease, disorientation, something wrong | Horizontal lines become diagonals. Restraint essential. | *The Third Man* — canted angles |

| `REL-FAVORING` | Favoring | Two in frame, camera clearly favors one | The camera has chosen. Whose scene this is. | Favored subject upper two-thirds. Other at edge. | *Succession* — favoring Logan |
| `REL-FACE-TO-FACE` | Face to Face | Two profiles facing each other | Confrontation, intimacy, negotiation. The gap IS tension. | Stack: upper third and lower third. The gap between is the scene. | *Heat* — Pacino and De Niro |
| `REL-BACKS` | Both Backs | Two from behind, facing away | Shared direction. Looking at the same thing. United or parallel. | Two silhouettes. World ahead fills upper frame. | *Lost in Translation* — looking out at Tokyo |

| `REL-TOUCH` | The Touch | Two people at the moment of physical contact — hand on shoulder, hands holding, embrace | The physical connection IS the cut. | `REL-DISTANCE` | The Distance | Two people in the same frame but separated by notable space | Emotional distance made spatial. The gap IS the diagnosis. | One figure top, one bottom. The empty middle is the relationship. | *Marriage Story* — hallway confrontation |
