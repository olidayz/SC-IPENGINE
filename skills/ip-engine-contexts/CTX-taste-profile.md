# Daniel's Taste Profile

A comprehensive model of creative taste spanning film, music, television, and brands. The quality filter for all IP Engine output.

**Deep reference:** For the complete genre-organized catalog of 350+ films and 60+ TV shows, consult [references/film-tv-references.md](references/film-tv-references.md).

---

## The Daniel Aesthetic: Integrated Summary

**Scandinavian-Japanese restraint** meets **American creative energy**. Things that are *considered* but not *precious*, *elevated* but not *exclusionary*. Craft should be visible — whether in a Fincher frame, a Kanye beat, or a Teenage Engineering synth. Moral complexity is preferred over moral clarity; audiences are trusted to do the work. Genre is a container for ideas, not a formula to follow. Comedy should have teeth — awkwardness, transgression, and honesty over setup-punchline. World-building matters across all media — inhabit the thing, don't just consume it. The space between mass and niche, where quality, accessibility, and cultural credibility overlap. Trust curators (A24, Criterion, Supreme) and systems thinkers (Apple, Figma). The through-line is **intentionality** — someone made choices, took positions, said no to things.

### Key Tensions That Define This Taste

| Tension | How It Manifests |
|---------|------------------|
| **Dark but not cynical** | Moral complexity with humanist undercurrent |
| **Weird but not inaccessible** | Demna yes, but there's a ceiling |
| **Premium but not exclusive** | Quality without pretension |
| **Playful but rigorous** | Teenage Engineering, Rick and Morty, LEGO Movie |
| **Nostalgic but forward** | Criterion Collection + Arc Browser |
| **Authored but collaborative** | Auteur theory meets Pixar/A24 models |

## Taste Principles

Twelve principles. Each one names the value, gives the diagnostic test, identifies what kills it, and points to references across media. This is the taste filter — everything passes or fails here.

---

### 1. Craft Obsession `#craftobsession`

The hand of the maker is visible. Intentionality legible in every frame, beat, and detail. The *how* matters as much as the *what*.

- **Test:** Can you feel who made this and why they made every choice?
- **Kills it:** Design by committee — no fingerprints, no soul. Heritage without innovation — resting on legacy.
- **Touchstone:** Playful but rigorous (Teenage Engineering + Wes Anderson)
- **Across media:** Nolan, PTA, Fincher (film) · Kanye, Pharrell, Radiohead (music) · Apple, Teenage Engineering, Porsche (brands) · Vince Gilligan, David Simon (TV)

---

### 2. Moral Weight `#moralweight`

Complexity over clarity. Antiheroes, compromised figures, situational ethics. Trust the audience to sit with ambiguity.

- **Test:** Does it respect the audience's intelligence? Does it trust ambiguity?
- **Kills it:** Moral simplicity — "This thinks I'm stupid." Manipulation over meaning — Oscar bait, emotional exploitation.
- **Touchstone:** Dark but accessible (Severance + A24)
- **Across media:** Antiheroes throughout (film) · Lyrical depth, Jay-Z, Kanye (music) · Walter White, Tony Soprano, the Roys (TV)

---

### 3. World Integrity `#worldbuilding`

Immersive, coherent worlds you can inhabit. Gravity matters. The world should feel bigger than any single story told inside it.

- **Test:** Could you spend time in this world? Does it have gravity? Does it generate stories beyond the one you're told?
- **Kills it:** Setting as wallpaper. Pretty without explorable depth.
- **Touchstone:** Worldbuilt but grounded (Miyazaki + The Wire)
- **Across media:** Wes Anderson, Miyazaki, Villeneuve (film) · Frank Ocean, Radiohead (music) · Soho House, Aesop (brands) · Game of Thrones, Dark (TV)

---

### 4. Authorial Voice `#authorvoice`

Singular vision. Unmistakable fingerprints. Someone is in control and has a point of view. Curation is creation — the act of selection is an act of authorship.

- **Test:** Is there a point of view? Could only this creator have made this?
- **Kills it:** Safe, consensus-chasing choices — "This is afraid of itself." Committee decisions — no one made a call.
- **Touchstone:** Rebellious but crafted (Supreme + Radiohead)
- **Across media:** Auteur cinema (film) · Artist-as-brand (music) · Supreme, A24 (brands) · Showrunner-driven (TV)

---

### 5. Emotional Specificity `#earnedemotion`

Precise feeling, not vague sentiment. Earned, not manufactured. Sincerity that knows it's sincere — without tipping into manipulation or ironic detachment.

- **Test:** Is the feeling precise? Or reaching for cheap catharsis?
- **Kills it:** Sentimentality without specificity — "This is manipulating me, not moving me." Irony without stakes — detachment as defense mechanism.
- **Touchstone:** Epic but intimate (Dune + Portrait of a Lady on Fire)
- **Across media:** Earned emotion (film) · Neo-soul precision (music) · Le Labo, Michelin (brands) · The Wire's realism (TV)

---

### 6. Genre Intelligence `#genresubversion`

Genre is a container for ideas, not a formula to follow. Honor conventions while transcending them. Use familiar forms to smuggle in something unexpected.

- **Test:** If it's genre, is it using the container to say something?
- **Kills it:** Formula without subversion — "I've seen this before, and not in a good way."
- **Touchstone:** Weird but welcoming (EEAAO + Liquid Death)
- **Across media:** Subversion within form (film) · Hip-hop evolution (music) · Liquid Death, Tesla (brands) · True Detective, Fargo (TV)

---

### 7. Accessible Depth `#accessibility`

Quality without pretension. Weird enough to be interesting, accessible enough to succeed. The space between mass and niche where cultural credibility and wide appeal overlap.

- **Test:** Is it quality without pretension? Can someone unfamiliar still enter?
- **Kills it:** Complexity for complexity's sake — pretension without payoff. Premium without purpose — expensive for expensive's sake. Exclusivity as value — status signaling over substance.
- **Touchstone:** Premium but democratic (Apple + Uniqlo)
- **Across media:** Blockbusters with ideas (film) · Pop with substance (music) · IKEA, Muji, Uniqlo (brands) · Prestige TV's mainstream success (TV)

---

### 8. Kinetic Tension `#tension` `#kineticenergy`

Pressure that builds deliberately. Movement as meaning. Earned release — or deliberate denial of release. Energy that serves the story, not spectacle.

- **Test:** Does it build pressure deliberately? Does it earn its moments?
- **Kills it:** Loudness without substance — spectacle over story. Safe middle ground — neither bold nor minimal, just... there.
- **Touchstone:** Chaotic but precise (The Bear + Uncut Gems)
- **Across media:** Mad Max, Whiplash (film) · Afrobeat, hip-hop (music) · Nike, Equinox (brands) · The Bear's kitchen chaos (TV)

---

### 9. Cultural Rootedness `#culturalspecificity`

Geographic DNA. Specificity of place, community, and lived experience. Universal meaning that can only emerge from local detail.

- **Test:** Does this feel like it comes from somewhere real? Is the specificity earned?
- **Kills it:** Trend-chasing — will be embarrassing in 3 years. Generic cosmopolitanism with no actual roots.
- **Touchstone:** Sophisticated but warm (In the Mood for Love + Aesop)
- **Across media:** City of God, Parasite (film) · Fela Kuti, Buena Vista Social Club (music) · Momofuku (brands) · The Wire's Baltimore (TV)

---

### 10. Visual Systems `#visualsystems`

Distinctive aesthetics with internal logic — maximalist or restrained, never default. The visual language should be inseparable from the content. Form serves function, and both serve beauty.

- **Test:** Does it work *and* look right? Could this visual language belong to anything else?
- **Kills it:** Visual/sonic blandness — "This looks/sounds like everything else."
- **Touchstone:** Minimalist but not cold (Muji + Frank Ocean)
- **Across media:** Wes Anderson's symmetry, Spider-Verse's animation (film) · HAY's color palette (brands)

---

### 11. Comedy with Teeth `#darkhumor`

Deadpan, discomfort-driven, with something real underneath. Awkwardness and transgression over setup-punchline. Earnest at its core — humor as a vehicle for truth, not a shield from it.

- **Test:** Is this funny *and* about something? Does the humor come from specificity, not cruelty or formula?
- **Kills it:** Humor without stakes. Comedy that's afraid to be sincere.
- **Touchstone:** Absurd but sincere (Nathan for You + Fleabag)
- **Across media:** Nathan for You, Fleabag, Curb, It's Always Sunny (TV) · Kanye's absurdism (music) · Wes Anderson (film)

---

### 12. Playful Engineering `#playfulengineering`

Serious tools with personality. Precision married to delight. The intersection of systems thinking and creative joy — where rigor becomes play.

- **Test:** Does it combine technical sophistication with genuine delight? Is the complexity joyful?
- **Kills it:** Seriousness without play. Play without rigor.
- **Touchstone:** Kinetic but intelligent (Whiplash + Kanye production)
- **Across media:** Spider-Verse, LEGO Movie (film) · Kanye's production (music) · Teenage Engineering, Figma (brands) · Rick and Morty (TV)

## Collision Mode: Taste × Mass Audience

### The Core Distinction

This taste is anti-mass-PROCESS, not anti-mass-OUTCOME. Dune ($700M+), Spider-Verse, Miyazaki, and Pixar are mass outcomes from specific visions. The filter rejects committee-built, audience-tested-to-death content — not mass audiences themselves.

| | Specific Vision | Consensus Vision |
|---|---|---|
| **Mass Outcome** | Dune, Pixar, Spider-Verse, Miyazaki, Matrix — **TARGET** | Transformers, most MCU — what the filter rejects |
| **Niche Outcome** | Cult classics, arthouse — respected but not the goal | Committee content that also flopped |

### Collision Mechanics

When building franchise-scale IP, collide this taste profile with what mass audiences need. Don't average them — find the overlap.

| Dimension | Taste Says | Mass Audiences Need | Collision = |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------|-------------|
| **Craft** | Visible intentionality | Invisible, serving feeling | Craft so good it becomes invisible — then you notice (Pixar, Miyazaki) |
| **Morality** | Complex, ambiguous | Clear, rootable | Complex WORLD + clear CHARACTER journey (WALL-E, Spider-Verse, Spirited Away) |
| **World** | Systems-first, robust | Secondary to character | World accessed THROUGH a character you love (Avatar: TLA, One Piece) |
| **Tone** | Dark but not cynical | Bright and warm | Wonder with weight — joy that acknowledges darkness and often chooses light (Soul, EEAAO, Up) |
| **Humor** | Dry, discomfort, teeth | Warm, inclusive | Warmth with surprise — from character specificity, not cruelty or formula (Lego Movie, Shrek, Rick and Morty) |
| **Scale** | Multi-decade infrastructure | Seasonal, returns | Deepens with each return — ages with its audience (Toy Story, Harry Potter) |

### Collision Benchmarks

IPs that pass BOTH this taste filter AND mass audience needs:

Pixar (best of), Miyazaki, Spider-Verse, The Matrix, Dune, EEAAO, WALL-E, Star Wars (original), Mad Max: Fury Road, Soul, Inception, The Dark Knight, Game of Thrones (peak)

## IP Engine Integration

The Taste Profile is **always on** — it passively informs all output at every stage.

Active invocation:
- "Filter this for taste" / "Would this pass the taste filter?" — run all 12 principles
- "Pull #[tag] references" — get examples for a specific principle
- "What's the touchstone for [vibe]?" — pattern-match an IP concept
- "Run collision mode" — test IP concept against both taste AND mass audience needs

**Critical for IP Engine:** The Collision Mode section is the most important part for franchise-scale IP. Every concept must target the upper-left quadrant: **specific vision → mass outcome**.
