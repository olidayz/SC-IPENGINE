# 4C-LOCATIONS

Stage 2b of visual development. Apply the VLD to spaces — what the world's key locations look like, feel like, sound like.

Runs in parallel with 4b-characters.md. Both constrained by the approved VLD from 4a.

---

## Anti-Patterns (Read First)

Kill any location visual package that matches these before presenting:

| Anti-Pattern | Signal | Why It Dies |
|-------------|--------|------------|
| **The Soundstage** | Location feels like a set, not a place. Clean, arranged, no evidence of life. | Add the mundane details. What's in the trash cans? What's on the bulletin board? What's the graffiti say? A location that's too "designed" breaks immersion. |
| **The Postcard** | Beautiful establishing shot, nothing specific underneath. Looks great from far away, generic up close. | Every location needs texture at three distances — wide (atmosphere), medium (human-scale), close-up (tactile detail). If only the wide works, it's a backdrop, not a location. |
| **The Exposition Room** | Location exists only to deliver information. The museum, the archive, the briefing room. Functional but lifeless. | Every space has a life beyond its plot function. What happens here when the protagonist isn't around? Who else uses this space, and for what? |
| **The Clone** | Two locations in the same faction territory look identical. No individual character. | Same faction doesn't mean same space. A GBA headquarters and a GBA borough outpost share visual DNA but have completely different textures, scales, and states of repair. |
| **The Ambient Void** | Location has visual design but no sensory presence. No sound, no smell, no temperature. | This is a WRITTEN skill — we can encode every sense. Downstream stages (5-scenes for description, 9-audio for sound design) need sensory direction beyond visual. |

### Process Anti-Pattern

| Anti-Pattern | Signal | Why It Dies |
|-------------|--------|------------|
| **The Open Kitchen** | Presenting principle analysis, generation prompts, evaluation tables to the user | The user sees location visual packages, not the generation process. |

## The 6 Principles of Environmental Design

### Principle 1: HISTORY LAYER

*Every space has a past visible in its present. The location is a palimpsest.*

Architecture, surfaces, signage, infrastructure — they all carry temporal information. A location's history isn't backstory to be told; it's visible in the frame.

| Prompt | Ask the Location |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Previous Life** | What was this space before it was what it is now? A church that became a nightclub. A factory that became lofts. A battlefield that became a park. The previous use leaves physical traces. |
| **Architectural Fossil** | What structural elements survive from an earlier era? An ornate ceiling above a drop-tile grid. A bank vault behind a coffee shop counter. Bones of the old life inside the new. |
| **Erasure** | What has someone tried to cover up or demolish? The effort of erasure is itself visible — fresh paint over old murals, new signage bolted over old brackets, demolished wings leaving scars on standing walls. |

**Benchmark:** *Chernobyl* (HBO) — the Pripyat amusement park, built for a May Day celebration that never happened. Brand-new rides covered in radioactive dust. The Time Signature is frozen: everything is the age it was at the moment of catastrophe, then entropy takes over unevenly.

### Principle 2: LIVED-IN TEST

*The mundane details that make a space feel inhabited, not designed.*

The difference between a SET and a PLACE is evidence of life. Sets are clean, logical, arranged. Places are messy, accumulated, personal.

| Prompt | Ask the Location |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Detritus** | What do people leave behind here? Coffee cups, cigarette butts, lost gloves, forgotten flyers, worn paths in grass? The waste products of human presence. |
| **Customization** | How have the inhabitants modified this space? Posters, plants, curtains, furniture rearrangement, tape marks, stickers, scratched initials? Personal marks on institutional surfaces. |
| **Malfunction** | What doesn't work? The flickering light, the stuck window, the door that doesn't lock, the stain that won't come out. Every space has its failures, and people have learned to live with them. |

**Benchmark:** *The Wire* — the Major Crimes Unit detail. The terrible office they're given: bad chairs, wrong desks, a wall of photos and string. It looks like people actually work there 16 hours a day because that's how it was designed — from the inside out, not the outside in.

### Principle 3: SCALE AS EMOTION

*How big the space feels relative to the people in it. Architecture is emotional.*

Scale communicates power, isolation, intimacy, oppression, grandeur, claustrophobia. The relationship between human body and surrounding space IS an emotional statement.

| Prompt | Ask the Location |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Power Ratio** | Does this space make people feel big or small? High ceilings and vast lobbies say: you are minor. Low ceilings and narrow halls say: you are contained. Which emotional register does this space operate in? |
| **Compression/Release** | Is this a space people enter to feel compressed (pressure, tension, confinement) or released (relief, freedom, expansion)? The transition between compressed and released spaces is cinematic. |
| **Occupancy** | How many people should be in this space vs. how many are? An empty ballroom, a packed elevator, a single person in a stadium — the occupancy ratio changes the emotional meaning. |

**Benchmark:** *Blade Runner 2049* — Wallace Corp's chamber is vast, empty, water-light rippling on massive walls, one man in it. The scale says: this person owns so much space that a single room dwarfs normal architecture. Contrast with K's apartment: human-scale, efficient, one window. Scale IS the class system made spatial.

### Principle 4: FACTION SIGNATURE

*Spaces declare territory. You know whose world you're in from the environment alone.*

Factions don't just occupy space — they transform it. The visual environment should tell you whose territory you're in before anyone speaks.

| Prompt | Ask the Location |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Material Language** | What materials does this faction use? Corporate factions favor glass, steel, clean surfaces. Street factions favor repurposed materials, spray paint, improvised infrastructure. The materials ARE the value system. |
| **Maintenance Level** | How well-maintained is this space? Pristine = resources + control. Decaying = neglect or resistance. Selectively maintained (clean lobby, filthy back rooms) = performance of order. |
| **Branding** | How does the faction mark its territory? Logos, colors, signage, uniforms, architecture itself? Is the branding subtle (embedded in design) or overt (flags and banners)? |
| **Border** | Where does this faction's territory end and another's begin? Is there a hard border (walls, checkpoints, different pavement) or a gradient (the neighborhood slowly changes)? Border zones are the most visually interesting spaces. |

**Benchmark:** *Parasite* — the Park house and the Kim semi-basement are the same city, but factionally distinct through every surface. The Parks: imported stone, manicured lawn, vast glass, automated lighting. The Kims: raw concrete, exposed pipes, street-level windows, manual everything. You know whose world you're in from the WALLS.

### Principle 5: THRESHOLD MOMENT

*Transitions between spaces are cinematic events. Every world has boundaries — crossing them is a shot.*

The most visually powerful moments in cinema are often TRANSITIONS — moving from one space to another, one territory to another, one reality to another. The VLD should design these crossings.

| Prompt | Ask the Location |
|--------|-----------------|
| **The Crossing** | What does the transition between this space and the adjacent one look and feel like? Is it abrupt (hard cut between worlds) or gradual (slow drift from one palette to another)? |
| **The Barrier** | What separates this space from the next? A door, a bridge, a border, an elevator, a tunnel, a staircase? The barrier itself tells a story — who controls it, who can pass, what's the cost of crossing? |
| **The Tell** | What's the first visual signal that you've entered a new territory? The material change, the light shift, the temperature difference, the sound shift? Name the earliest possible signal. |

**Benchmark:** *Parasite* — the staircase descent. From the Parks' hilltop garden to the Kims' flooded semi-basement. Each step changes the material, the light, the water level. The threshold isn't a door — it's a topographic gradient. You DESCEND through class. The transition IS the thesis.

### Principle 6: SENSORY DEPTH

*Beyond visual — what does this space sound like, smell like, feel like?*

This is a written skill, not an image generator. We can encode every sense. Downstream stages need sensory direction: 5-scenes for script description, 9-audio for sound design, 7-motion for atmospheric effects.

| Prompt | Ask the Location |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Acoustic Character** | What does this space SOUND like? Echo and reverb (hard surfaces, large volumes), or muffled and dead (soft surfaces, small rooms)? What's the ambient sound — machinery, traffic, wind, silence, fluorescent hum? |
| **Smell Signature** | What does this space SMELL like? Industrial chemicals, old paper, cooking, rain on concrete, new carpet, disinfectant? Smell is the fastest route to memory and emotional response. |
| **Tactile Quality** | What would you FEEL touching the surfaces? Cold metal, warm wood, gritty concrete, smooth glass, sticky countertops? What's the temperature? Humidity? Air quality? |

**Benchmark:** *The Bear* — the kitchen is designed in every sense. The SOUND: ticket printer, sizzle, yelling, clattering metal, the specific acoustic of a cramped hot space with hard surfaces. The SMELL: implied through dialogue ("it smells like a f***ing zoo") and food details. The FEEL: heat, grease, the wetness of prep, the cold of the walk-in. It's a sensorium, not just a set.

## Generation Process

### Step 1: Identify Key Locations

From the story window + world build: 5-8 locations that the narrative needs. Include at least one per faction territory, the protagonist's primary space, and one threshold/border location.

### Step 2: For Each Location — Run the Principles

Work through the 6 principles in order. The VLD constrains every decision — palette must come from the VLD's color system, materials from the VLD's texture rules, lighting from the VLD's philosophy.

**Generation order per location:**

1. **Faction Signature** — whose territory, material language, maintenance, branding
2. **History Layer** — previous life, architectural fossils, erasures
3. **Scale as Emotion** — power ratio, compression/release, occupancy
4. **Lived-In Test** — detritus, customization, malfunction
5. **Threshold Moment** — how you enter and exit, what the crossing looks and feels like
6. **Sensory Depth** — sound, smell, touch, temperature, air

### Step 3: Spatial Coherence Check (Internal)

Do the locations work as a visual system? Test:
- Do faction territories read as distinct through environment alone?
- Is there enough visual contrast between locations in different registers?
- Do the thresholds between spaces create visual events?
- Does the protagonist's primary space reflect their character (or deliberately contrast with it)?

### Step 4: Gate Check (Internal — Do Not Present)

Run each location against quality gates. Fix failures before presenting.

## Output Structure

Stage 2 location output uses the two-layer structure defined in SKILL.md:

### Layer 1: Location Overview Table (present FIRST)

Scannable checkpoint + visual sampling. One row per location. User reviews this before proceeding. The Image Prompt column contains a ready-to-use generation prompt.

```
| Location | Territory | Register | Key Visual Idea | Threshold | Image Prompt |
|----------|-----------|----------|----------------|-----------|-------------|
| **[Name]** | [faction or contested] | [VLD tonal register] | [one-sentence visual hook] | [the crossing — what changes when you enter] | [Single establishing shot prompt. Architecture, palette, lighting, key textures, emotional register, VLD style anchor. Self-contained, 80-120 words, includes anti-prompt.] |
```

### Layer 2: Full Location Visual Packages

One per location. Complete depth — every principle covered.

```
## [LOCATION NAME]

**Territory:** [which faction, or contested/neutral]
**Narrative Function:** [what happens here in the story]
**Emotional Register:** [which of the VLD's tonal registers this space operates in]

**Establishing Shot:** [how the audience first sees this place — framing, time of day, approach angle]

**Palette & Lighting:** [how the VLD manifests in this specific space — colors, light sources, shadow behavior]

**Architecture & Scale:** [style, era, condition, size relative to people, power ratio]

**History Layer:** [what this space used to be, architectural fossils, visible erasures]

**Texture & Detail:** [surfaces up close — materials, wear, detritus, customization, malfunction]

**Sensory Depth:**
- **Sound:** [acoustic character, ambient noise, signature sounds]
- **Smell:** [smell signature — what hits you when you walk in]
- **Feel:** [temperature, humidity, air quality, tactile surfaces]

**Set Dressing:** [key environmental objects that tell the story of this place — what's on desks, walls, streets, tables]

**Faction Signature:** [how you know whose territory this is — materials, maintenance, branding, borders]

**Threshold:** [how you enter/exit — the crossing, the barrier, the first visual tell]
```

## Quality Gates (Run Silently)

| Gate | Test | Kill If |
|------|------|---------|
| **Taste Filter** | Does this location pass Daniel's aesthetic PASS/FAIL? | Generic, seen-in-every-movie, soundstage energy |
| **VLD Compliance** | Does the location follow the VLD's rules? | Colors, materials, or lighting that violate the established visual language |
| **Specificity** | Is this location specific to THIS world? | Could be any office / any alley / any headquarters |
| **Lived-In** | Does the space feel inhabited? | Too clean, too arranged, too "designed" — the Soundstage problem |
| **Sensory Completeness** | Are senses beyond visual addressed? | No sound, no smell, no temperature — an Ambient Void |
| **Principle Coverage** | Are all 6 principles addressed? | Any principle completely skipped = incomplete design |

**If all pass:** Present location visual packages.

**If any fail:** Fix silently, then present.
