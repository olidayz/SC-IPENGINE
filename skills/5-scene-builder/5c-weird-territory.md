# 5c — WEIRD TERRITORY (Invocation 3)

The obvious ideas are spent. The material has been mined. The entities have been invented. Now push into the territory nobody thought of — the scenes that make you pause and say "I would never have generated that."

This invocation's quality bar is SURPRISE. If a scene could have been generated in Invocations 1 or 2, it fails. Gate 6 (Promise Break) is MANDATORY for every scene.

## Story Precedence

The story window from 3-stories is your primary narrative source. Weird territory means unexpected ANGLES on the world — not contradicting the story's decisions. Push into strange corners, unseen perspectives, and format collisions, but the story's core beats and character arcs remain intact.

## The Three Forcing Functions

Every scene in this invocation uses at least one forcing function. Many will use two or three simultaneously. The forcing functions are generative prompts, not categories — they push you into territory that pure extraction and entity-matching can't reach.

### FORCING FUNCTION 1: COLLISION

Smash two things together that don't naturally belong in the same frame. The tension between the two elements creates novelty. The collision must feel inevitable in retrospect — "of course these two things would meet in this world."

**Collision types:**

| Collision | What You Smash | What You Get |
|---|---|---|
| **Instrument × Instrument** | Fuse two Cultural Instruments into one scene. Therapy session × cooking show. Security cam × ASMR. News broadcast × lullaby. | A format that didn't exist before. The friction between the two containers creates a new container. |
| **Location × Location** | Put a scene at the BORDER between two locations, or transplant one location's activity into another location's space. | The managed world leaking into the unmanaged world, or vice versa. The moment the map's edges touch. |
| **Character × Character** | Generate a scene with two characters who NEVER interact in the story — whose worldviews, aesthetics, and spatial vocabularies are maximally different. | The visual collision when two faction aesthetics share one frame. The temperature change when two registers meet. |
| **Format × Reality** | A media artifact (from Invocation 2) breaking the fourth wall — the format failing, the mask slipping, the production going wrong. A corporate video where the talent looks into the wrong camera. A news broadcast where the anchor cracks. | The most human moment is when the format can't contain the person inside it. |
| **Time × Time** | A single frame containing two time periods — the world before and after its defining event. The old sign under the new sign. The original architecture under the renovation. The fossil inside the new construction. | Temporal collision in one image. History as palimpsest. |

### FORCING FUNCTION 2: GENRE INVERSION

Take the world's established tonal registers and FLIP them. If the world defaults to corporate absurdism, play a scene with devastating sincerity. If a location is coded as warm, show it cold. Break the tonal contract the world has established with its audience.

**Inversion types:**

| Inversion | What You Flip | What You Get |
|---|---|---|
| **Tone flip** | The world's default register, reversed. Corporate absurdism played as genuine tragedy. Noir played as warm comedy. The sacred space played as mundane. The mundane space played as sacred. | The audience's expectations are the raw material. Subverting them reveals something the default register hides. |
| **Scale flip** | The epic made intimate. The intimate made epic. A world-changing event seen from a window. A personal moment given the visual weight of a state funeral. | Scale mismatch creates emotional dissonance — and dissonance creates attention. |
| **Format flip** | A media format whose contract with the viewer is broken. The wholesome format carrying horrific content. The urgent format treating something mundane as emergency. The instructional format teaching something impossible. | When the format and the content disagree, the viewer's brain resolves the tension by paying closer attention. |
| **Beauty flip** | The ugly space made beautiful. The beautiful space made ugly. Golden-hour light on industrial decay. Fluorescent light on a garden. | Lighting and composition can make anything gorgeous or repulsive. Invert the expected treatment. |

### FORCING FUNCTION 3: THE UNSEEN

The world package describes the visible world. This forcing function generates scenes from the INVISIBLE parts — the blind spots, the margins, the taboo, the temporal cracks, the sensory dimensions nobody documented.

**Unseen lenses:**

**TEMPORAL UNSEEN** — What does this world look like at 3:47 AM? During the boring hours between shifts? On a holiday when the systems are off? The first minute of the new year? The last minute of the old order? Time is a lens — point it at the unused hours.

**PERIPHERAL UNSEEN** — What do the background characters experience? The janitor in the institution. The cab driver who drives between all factions. The child too young to understand the system they live in. The elderly person who remembers the world before. The tourist who arrived yesterday.

**TABOO UNSEEN** — What does this world NOT talk about? Every civilization has subjects it avoids, contradictions it papers over, hypocrisies it maintains. The scene is the moment the taboo surfaces — in a whisper, a glance, a sign someone forgot to take down.

**SENSORY UNSEEN** — What does this world SMELL like? TASTE like? Feel against your skin? The world package covers sight and sound. Push into the other senses and translate them into visual+audio fragments. The taste of the coffee in the institutional break room. The temperature shift in the threshold. The texture of the hand-stitched versus the injection-molded.

**DREAM UNSEEN** — What do the characters in this world dream about? What do children dream about? What do the old guard dream about? Dreams are visual logic at its most compressed — a scene from a dream carries more emotional density per frame than a scene from waking life.

**NON-HUMAN UNSEEN** — What does this world look like from a non-human perspective? The animal that lives in the margins. The surveillance camera that sees everything. The architecture itself — what would the building say if it could speak? The weather as a character. The signal as a character.

**SYSTEMIC UNSEEN** — What does the world's infrastructure look like from inside? The server room. The plumbing. The electrical grid. The database. The filing cabinet. Systems have physical manifestations — the pipes under the polished floor, the wires behind the wall, the code behind the screen.

## Mix and Intensity

This invocation has no mix target. Follow the weird. The balance between cinematic and world media should emerge from which forcing functions produce the strongest scenes. Some of the best weird-territory scenes will be collisions BETWEEN cinematic and world media — a format breaking down, a media artifact appearing in a physical space, a character watching their own representation on a screen.

**Intensity guidance:** Push to the edge of the world's tonal range, but don't break it. The scene should feel like it belongs in this world — just in a corner of it nobody had looked at before. If a scene feels like it's from a DIFFERENT IP, it's gone too far. If it feels comfortable and familiar, it hasn't gone far enough.

## Anti-Patterns (Invocation 3 Specific)

| Anti-Pattern | Signal |
|---|---|
| **Weird for weird's sake** | The scene is bizarre but doesn't reveal anything about the world. Strangeness must carry world-building payload. Surrealism is cheap; surrealism that MEANS something is expensive. |
| **Retreating to safety** | Generating scenes that could have come from Invocations 1 or 2. The Exclusion Lists exist to prevent this. If the scene feels familiar, kill it. |
| **Losing the perspective** | Weird-territory scenes still need a clear point of view. The dream has dream logic. The non-human perspective has its own way of seeing. The collision has a frame that contains both elements. |
| **Losing the world** | The most common failure mode. Weird-territory scenes must still feel like they're FROM this specific world. The world's rules, textures, materials, and faction dynamics should be present even in the strangest scene. |
| **All collisions, no unseen** | Collisions are the easiest forcing function. The Unseen produces the most surprising scenes but requires more imaginative effort. Push into The Unseen when collision ideas dry up. |
| **All surface, no texture** | Weird-territory scenes need sensory specificity. A dream has a material quality. A non-human perspective has its own sense of temperature and surface. Don't let the weirdness stay abstract. |
