# 5a — EXTRACT + EXPAND (Invocation 1)

Work from what exists. Mine the world package for every cinematic moment — explicit, implied, and lurking in the margins. Then expand into new scenes the material points toward but never describes.

This invocation produces the backbone: the scenes most directly rooted in the world's established characters, locations, conflicts, and story beats. Both cinematic fragments AND world media scenes using Cultural Instruments.

**Naming rule:** Use character and location names from the Name Registry in every hook line. Never role labels ("the protagonist"), never generic descriptions ("the canyon"). Names carry forward into 6-shots prompts.

---

## Story Precedence

The story window from 3-stories is your primary narrative source. The world bible provides setting, factions, systems, and texture — but where the world bible implies story beats that conflict with the developed story, the story window wins. Extract world-building scenes from the bible freely. Extract story-driven scenes from the story window. Don't let a world bible implication override a story decision.

---

## Before Generating: Spine Candidates

Read the full world package. Identify 4-6 spine candidates — the world's strongest recurring threads. These will be carried forward across all five invocations but are NEVER used as generation prompts. Best scenes first. The Spine Report at the end of all invocations will analyze which spines actually emerged strongest.

Output spines with one-sentence descriptions before generating any scenes.

---

## Extraction Patterns

Systematically scan each section of the world package. Every section contains scenes hiding in the prose.

### From Locations (VLD + Location Packages)

| What to Extract | Where It Hides | Scene It Becomes |
|---|---|---|
| **Establishing images** | The "Establishing Shot" paragraph in each location package | The image that DEFINES this space — the first thing you'd see. |
| **Thresholds** | The "Threshold" section — the crossing between zones | The moment of environmental shift. The threshold IS the scene. |
| **Set dressing details** | Buried in texture/detail paragraphs | A single object, isolated, carrying the whole world. The most specific detail becomes the most universal image. |
| **Sensory moments** | The "Sensory Depth" sections — sound, smell, feel | Translate a sensory description into a scene idea. What does "fluorescent buzz" feel like as a moment? |
| **Time layers** | The "History Layer" sections — traces of previous eras | The moment where two time periods collide in one image: old architecture wearing new signage, original fixtures holding new light. |
| **The wrong detail** | Anything that feels incongruous within a location | The gift shop at the end of the alley. The QR code on the sacred object. The branded padlock next to the rusted one. Incongruity is the scene. |

### From Characters (Character Visual Packages)

| What to Extract | Where It Hides | Scene It Becomes |
|---|---|---|
| **Signature details** | The "Signature Detail" section per character | The detail in isolation — the watch, the badge, the drawer, the pin. |
| **Body in space** | The "Body in Space" section | The character's relationship to a room. Standing when others sit. Occupying edges. Sinking into a chair. |
| **Visual arc endpoints** | "Start" and "End" in the Visual Arc section | Two scenes per character — who they are at the start vs. the end. The delta between the two. |
| **Costume logic** | Primary state vs. secondary state | The moment the armor comes off. The blazer removed. The transition between performed self and real self. |
| **The absence** | What the character DOESN'T carry, wear, or display | The empty hook. The bare wrist. The face-down photograph. Absence as portrait. |

### From Story (Story Window)

| What to Extract | Where It Hides | Scene It Becomes |
|---|---|---|
| **Pressure cooker beats** | Each named beat in the story structure | The IMAGE of that beat — not the narrative, but the single moment where the tension is highest. |
| **The defining choice** | The protagonist's key decision point | The moment BEFORE the choice — the face, the hands, the object, the silence. The precipice. |
| **Collisions** | Where two characters/factions physically meet | The threshold between them — who's standing, who's seated, what's between them. |
| **The cost** | What the protagonist loses or becomes | The final image of the character. What's different. What isn't. |

### From World Bible (Rules, Factions, Objects)

| What to Extract | Where It Hides | Scene It Becomes |
|---|---|---|
| **Rules in action** | The world's rules/laws/systems | The rule being followed, broken, or exploited. Not the rule itself — the human moment where the rule meets a person. |
| **Faction textures** | Each faction's territory, methods, aesthetic | The faction's visual signature distilled to one frame. The material culture — what they make, wear, carry, display. |
| **Objects/artifacts** | The world's significant objects | The object in context — who's holding it, where it sits, what's around it. Also: the object in WRONG context (a sacred thing in a gift shop, a weapon in a museum). |
| **Conflict surfaces** | Where factions contest the same resource/space/symbol | The physical manifestation of ideological conflict — two versions of the same symbol side by side. Two people in the same uniform on opposite sides. |

### From World Media (Cultural Instruments — Invocation 1)

Load `references/cultural-instruments.md`. Generate world media scenes using any of the three application modes:

**Entity-driven:** Scan the world package for any entity that produces media — a news outlet, a propaganda arm, a tourism board, a content calendar. Generate 1-3 scenes per entity as media artifacts intercepted from inside the civilization.

**Character-driven:** Any character can USE a format. A protagonist filming a confessional. A faction leader doing a GRWM. A background character recording a voice memo. The character's relationship to the format IS the scene.

**Format-inspired:** Browse the instrument library. When a format collides interestingly with this world's content, generate the scene — even if no specific entity or character was pre-identified. A PSA about this world's specific danger. A tutorial for this world's specific skill. An unboxing of this world's specific artifact.

In Invocation 1, lean toward entities and characters the material already names. Save bulk entity invention for Invocation 2. But don't let "named only" kill a great scene — if the instrument library sparks something brilliant, generate it.

## Expansion Patterns

After extraction, generate NEW scenes the material implies but doesn't state.

### Character Collisions

Which characters have never been in the same room — but SHOULD be? The protagonist and the person they'll never meet. Two faction leaders on opposite sides of the same symbol. The liminal character observing the powerful one. Generate the frame where they almost-meet, where one watches the other unknowingly, where their spaces overlap.

### Emotional Extremes

Push characters to their edges. What does the composed character look like at 3 AM? What does the institutional space look like when the power goes out? What does the sacred space look like when nobody's watching? The extremes reveal what the center hides.

### The Eye Finds

Wander through a location and NOTICE something the text described but never framed as a scene. A detail in a corner. A reflection in glass. A contrast between foreground and background. What would a curious observer linger on?

### Threshold Expansions

The VLD locations have thresholds. But thresholds also exist between: day and night in the same space. Before and after an event. The public face and the private interior. A character entering a space where they don't belong. Every threshold is a visual event.

### Object Journeys

Take a significant object and imagine it in a different context. The sacred artifact in a gift shop. The institutional badge on a kitchen counter. The hand-made thing in an evidence bag. Objects migrate through the world — each migration is a scene.

## Mix Target

~35 cinematic scenes (locations, characters, story, objects, thresholds)
~15 world media scenes (entity-driven, character-driven, or format-inspired)
Flexible — follow what the material gives you.

---

## After Generation

1. Review all ~50 scenes against the 7 Gates. Kill anything that fails.
2. Replace killed scenes to maintain ~50 count.
3. Output the Exclusion List — territory covered, angles explored, scene titles.

## Anti-Patterns (Invocation 1 Specific)

| Anti-Pattern | Signal |
|---|---|
| **Transcription, not transformation** | Copying prose from the world bible into the hook line. The skill's job is to REFRAME world material as scene ideas, not transcribe it. |
| **Character study over-indexing** | 20+ character scenes, 5 location scenes. The world is bigger than its protagonist. |
| **Story-beat literalism** | Generating the narrative beat instead of the IMAGE within it. The beat is "Ade discovers the tracking is theater." The scene is "a roster board with 340 green dots, all showing on-route, on the night a man died." |
| **Ignoring the VLD** | Generating scenes that violate the Visual Language Document's rules — wrong palette, wrong world texture. The VLD constrains. |
| **All drama, no texture** | Every scene is a peak emotional moment. The quiet scenes — a detail, a texture, an empty space — are often the most powerful fragments. |
