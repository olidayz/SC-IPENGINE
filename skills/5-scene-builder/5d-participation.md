# 5d — PARTICIPATION (Invocation 4)

Scenes designed for audience entry. The viewer sees this and thinks: "I want to DO that." "I want to OWN that." "I want to BE in that faction." This invocation generates the invitation layer — the scenes that turn spectators into participants.

---

## Story Precedence

The story window from 3-stories remains the primary narrative source. Participation scenes exist IN the world the story established — they don't contradict story decisions. But they extend the world's surface area to include the audience as inhabitants, not just observers.

## The Participation Principle

The best participation scenes are SPECIFIC. Not "fans could join a faction" but "a person wearing a Cauldron-variant cowl at a bar, and the person next to them recognizing the borough from the ear proportions." The specificity is what makes participation feel real — it gives the audience a concrete action, object, or identity to adopt.

**The scene must show the EXPERIENCE of participation, not the MECHANIC of participation.** Don't describe the system. Show what it feels like to be inside it.

## Participation Lenses

Use these lenses to generate scenes. Not all will apply to every world. Use the ones that produce the strongest scenes.

### WEARABLE IDENTITY

What do people in this world WEAR that signals faction, belief, status, or allegiance? Not costumes — wearable signals. A pin. A specific color. A variant of a key object. A piece of streetwear with faction coding. The wearable is the scene: someone wearing it in a context where it means something to the people around them.

### SIGNAL & RITUAL

What repeatable action can fans perform that echoes the world's rituals? Activating a signal. Making a specific gesture. Posting in a specific format. Gathering at a specific time. The ritual is the scene: someone performing it, alone or in a group, and the performance meaning something beyond the act.

### FACTION DECLARATION

Choosing a side is the oldest participation mechanic. But the scene isn't the choice — it's the CONSEQUENCE of the choice. The moment someone's faction affiliation is visible to others and others react. The social currency of allegiance. The argument about which faction is right, conducted through objects, clothing, and presence rather than debate.

### MAKING & CRAFT

What do fans MAKE in this world? Handmade versions of key objects. Custom variants. Art. Modified artifacts. The act of making is participation at its deepest — the maker's hands, the materials, the imperfect result that carries more meaning than the official version.

### LOCATION & PILGRIMAGE

Where would fans GO in this world? The real-world equivalent of the world's sacred spaces. The pilgrimage is the scene: someone arriving at a place that means something, the place looking different than expected, the gap between the mythology and the physical reality.

### USER-GENERATED CANON

What content do fans create that becomes part of the world? A local Batman for your real neighborhood. A borough declaration. A journal entry in the Archivist's format. The fan-created content is the scene: the moment of creation, the moment of sharing, the moment it gets recognized or rejected by the community.

### COLLECTION & DISPLAY

What do people collect from this world, and how do they display it? The objects on a shelf. The pins on a jacket. The variant editions. The display is a portrait of the collector — what they chose reveals who they are within the world's value system.

## World Media Scenes in Participation

Participation generates its own media artifacts:

- **Fan-created content** as a Cultural Instrument: the fan edit, the fan theory video, the cosplay GRWM, the faction argument thread
- **Official content designed for participation**: the faction quiz, the registration system, the choose-your-side prompt, the open-registry submission
- **Hybrid content**: fan-created work that gets canonized, official content that fans modify, the boundary between creator and audience dissolving

---

## Mix Target

~50 scenes. No fixed ratio between lenses — follow what the world supports. Some worlds are heavy on wearable identity (faction-driven worlds). Some are heavy on ritual (worlds with repeatable symbolic actions). Some are heavy on craft (worlds with hand-made vs. institutional tensions). Let the world's architecture determine the mix.

## Anti-Patterns (Invocation 4 Specific)

| Anti-Pattern | Signal |
|---|---|
| **Mechanic over experience** | Describing the participation SYSTEM instead of what it FEELS LIKE. "Fans can choose a faction" is a feature list. "A person in a Cauldron cowl and a person in a Burnley cowl arguing about the Violence Clause at a bar" is a scene. |
| **Merch catalog** | Listing purchasable objects without showing them IN CONTEXT. A cowl on a shelf is a product photo. A cowl on a hook next to someone's house keys is a world. |
| **Generic fandom** | Scenes that could describe participation in ANY IP. The specificity of THIS world's participation culture is the point. |
| **Excluding non-fans** | The best participation scenes also work for people who haven't joined anything yet. The scene should be compelling even if you don't know what the factions are — it should MAKE you want to know. |
| **All physical, no digital** | Participation happens online too. Forum posts, faction arguments, fan theories, signal activations aggregated on a map. The digital participation layer is as rich as the physical one. |
