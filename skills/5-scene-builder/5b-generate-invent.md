# 5b — GENERATE + INVENT (Invocation 2)

Work from what SHOULD exist but doesn't yet. The world material has been mined. Now generate new cinematic scenes the material never implies, AND invent the media entities this civilization would produce — then turn those entities into scene fragments.

This invocation produces the depth: scenes that prove the world is richer than its documented material, and media artifacts that prove the civilization is real.

**Naming rule:** Use character and location names from the Name Registry in every hook line. New characters or locations invented here get named immediately — they enter the registry.

---

## Story Precedence

The story window from 3-stories is your primary narrative source. The world bible provides setting, factions, systems, and texture — but where the world bible implies story beats that conflict with the developed story, the story window wins. New cinematic scenes should expand the world WITHOUT contradicting the story's decisions. If you invent a scene that reframes a story beat, check it against the story window first.

---

## Read the Exclusion List First

Before generating anything, read the Exclusion List from Invocation 1. Understand which territories, characters, locations, and angles have been covered. Every scene in this invocation must explore DIFFERENT ground.

## PART 1: CINEMATIC GENERATION (~25 scenes)

New scenes that no extraction from the material would find. The world bible describes the architecture. This section generates the life INSIDE that architecture — the moments between documented beats, the people in the margins, the details nobody wrote down.

### Generation Lenses

Use these lenses to push beyond the material. Not all will apply to every world. Use the ones that produce the strongest scenes.

**THE MOMENT BEFORE**
Every story beat has a moment before it — the pause, the preparation, the last second of the old reality. What does the space look like the minute before the inciting event? What does the character's face look like the second before the decision? The precipice is more cinematic than the fall.

**THE MOMENT AFTER**
The aftermath. The space after everyone leaves. The face after the news lands. The quiet that follows the noise. Post-event scenes carry the emotional weight of the event without needing to depict it.

**THE BYSTANDER**
The world bible follows protagonists. But every protagonist scene has witnesses — background figures, peripheral characters, people who see the world differently because they're not at its center. The bystander's perspective is a scene: what do they see? What do they feel? How does the world look from the edge of the frame?

**THE PARALLEL**
Somewhere in this world, someone is doing the mundane version of what the protagonist does dramatically. The institutional version of the personal crisis. The child's version of the adult's dilemma. The routine version of the extraordinary event. Parallels create scenes that rhyme with the story without being part of it.

**THE MATERIAL**
What is MADE in this world? What's manufactured, hand-crafted, printed, grown, cooked, assembled? The production of objects is always cinematic — hands working, machines running, materials transforming. The factory, the workshop, the kitchen, the studio, the print shop.

**THE INFRASTRUCTURE**
Who maintains this world? Who fixes the pipes, replaces the lights, cleans the floors, files the paperwork? Infrastructure workers see the world from inside its walls. Their scenes are texture-rich and carry implicit commentary about who the world serves.

**THE TRANSITION**
What happens between the documented time periods in the world's history? The material might describe Era A and Era B — but the transition between them is the scene. The last day of the old order. The first day of the new one. The renovation in progress. The sign being changed.

**THE PRIVATE MOMENT**
What do characters do when nobody's watching and the plot isn't advancing? The phone call. The mirror. The meal eaten alone. The walk home. Private moments reveal the person the public performance conceals.

## PART 2: WORLD MEDIA GENERATION

The richest generation territory. This world has a civilization, and civilizations produce media. Your job is to identify entities that produce media, invent new ones, AND use Cultural Instruments freely across all three application modes (entity-driven, character-driven, format-collision).

### Step 1: Scan for Existing Media Entities

Read the world package for any entity, institution, character, or system that is described as producing, broadcasting, publishing, recording, or distributing content. List them.

### Step 2: Invent New Media Entities

For each institutional category below, ask: "What media would this sector produce in this world?" Generate as many entities as the world can support — aim for 30-50+ total (found + invented).

| Institutional Category | What to Invent | Example Questions |
|---|---|---|
| **Government / Municipal** | Official communications, PSAs, training materials, public records, regulatory content, emergency broadcasts | What does the government TELL its citizens? What does it WARN about? What does it CELEBRATE? What does it HIDE? |
| **Corporate / Commercial** | Advertising, investor communications, brand content, recruitment, internal memos, luxury marketing | What does money sell here? What brand content exists? What does the corporate voice sound like? |
| **News / Journalism** | Broadcasts, investigative reports, opinion pieces, weather, sports, press conferences, field dispatches | What does the news cover? What stories does it miss? What bias does it carry? Who anchors it? |
| **Community / Grassroots** | Local media, community boards, neighborhood communications, mutual aid, protest, folk culture | What do neighborhoods tell each other? What's hand-made, photocopied, spray-painted? |
| **Entertainment** | Shows, podcasts, music, comedy, performances, influencer content, streaming | What do people in this world watch for fun? What's their escapism? What's their guilty pleasure? |
| **Education** | Schools, training programs, children's content, universities, vocational, tutorials | How are children taught? What does the textbook look like? What values does education transmit? |
| **Personal / Social** | Social media posts, dating profiles, reviews, voice memos, text threads, photo albums, home video | What do individuals share? What do they document? What do their phones contain? |
| **Religious / Philosophical** | Sermons, ceremonies, philosophical lectures, moral education, sacred texts, spiritual practice | What do people believe? How is belief transmitted? What rituals exist? |
| **Underground / Counter-culture** | Bootleg content, protest media, samizdat, unlicensed broadcasts, graffiti, manifestos | What does the resistance produce? What's illegal to say? What circulates in secret? |
| **Legal / Bureaucratic** | Court transcripts, permits, certificates, compliance materials, incident reports, waivers | What does the paperwork look like? What forms do people fill out? What does the fine print say? |
| **Medical / Therapeutic** | Health communications, therapy sessions, support groups, wellness content, pharmaceutical marketing | How does this world care for its people? What does healing look like? What does the waiting room contain? |
| **Historical / Archival** | Museum materials, documentary footage, oral histories, preservation efforts, academic analysis | How does this world remember itself? What's preserved? What's been rewritten? |

### Step 3: Generate World Media Scenes

With entities identified (found + invented) and `references/cultural-instruments.md` loaded, generate scenes through ALL three application modes:

**MODE A — Entity × Instrument:**
Match entities to Cultural Instruments where the format-content collision is most interesting. A government PSA about a specific fictional danger. A corporate training video that reveals institutional values through its blandness. A bootleg podcast from the underground. The entity's voice + the instrument's container = the scene.

**MODE B — Character × Instrument:**
Characters USE media formats. The protagonist doing a confessional. A faction member filming a haul of looted/traded/sacred objects. A child making a tutorial about something only children in this world would know. The character's relationship to the format reveals who they are outside the plot.

**MODE C — Instrument as Spark:**
Browse the instrument library for formats that collide with this world's content in ways no entity or character specifically demands. A nature documentary about this world's fauna. A review of this world's contested space. A dating profile from inside a faction. The instrument itself is the generation prompt — the world fills in the content.

Target 25-35 instruments total across all three modes. List each with its source (entity, character, or format-spark) and a one-line rationale.

**Prioritize instruments where the format IS the world-building.** A corporate training video from this world's dominant institution isn't just content — it reveals values, power structures, and blind spots through the FORMAT itself.

**Selection anti-patterns:**
- Don't select instruments whose format doesn't create tension with this world's content. A nature documentary only works if there's something worth documenting.
- Don't over-index on one application mode. If all 25 scenes are entity-driven, push into character-driven and format-spark modes.
- Don't over-index on one instrument category. If you have 10 News scenes and 2 Comedy scenes, rebalance.

## PART 3: WORLD MEDIA SCENE GENERATION (~25 scenes)

With entities identified and instruments matched, generate scenes. Each scene is a media ARTIFACT — a fragment of content produced by an entity in this world, as if intercepted from inside the civilization and leaked into ours.

### Scene-as-Artifact Principle

These scenes don't DEPICT the world from outside. They ARE media FROM INSIDE the world. The camera isn't a filmmaker's camera — it's the camera that exists within the civilization. A news broadcast has news-broadcast camera language. A security feed has security-feed quality. A phone recording has phone-recording shakiness. A corporate video has corporate-video polish.

The format's production values ARE the scene's visual language. A GBA training video is institutional-clean. An underground recruitment tape is bootleg-rough. A luxury brand ad is aspirational-polished. The production quality of the in-world media tells you about the entity that made it.

### Generation Process

For each entity + instrument combination:
1. What would this entity ACTUALLY produce using this format?
2. What world-building payload does the artifact carry? (The content reveals something about the world that straight exposition would take paragraphs to explain.)
3. What's the HOOK — what's the one-sentence image that captures the most striking moment of this artifact?
4. Apply 7 Gates. Kill if it fails.

## Anti-Patterns (Invocation 2 Specific)

| Anti-Pattern | Signal |
|---|---|
| **Entity without voice** | Generating a media artifact that doesn't feel like the entity that made it. A government PSA should feel like a government PSA, not like a filmmaker's impression of one. |
| **Format over content** | The scene is a good example of the format but doesn't reveal anything about THIS world. A "breaking news" scene that could be from any IP. |
| **Instrument without world** | Selecting instruments that don't create tension with THIS world's content. Every instrument scene must reveal something specific about this civilization — not just be a good example of the format. |
| **All world media, no cinematic** | Invocation 2 generates BOTH types. Don't let the entity generation process consume all the scene slots. Cinematic generation pushes the world's visual vocabulary forward. |
| **Redundancy with Invocation 1** | Generating scenes in territories the Exclusion List covers. Read it. Respect it. |
